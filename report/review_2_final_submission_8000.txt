HOSPITAL SHARED-ACCOUNT ELIMINATION & ACCOUNTABLE ACTION ATTRIBUTION
Project Review #2: 70% Completion Milestone Submission Report
Academic Proof-of-Concept | Coverage: 91.15% | Attribution: 99.02%

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Clinical environments use shared accounts (radiology_shared, er_triage_shared, icu_shared_ws, pharmacy_dispenser) to prevent login delays during urgent care. When sensitive actions occur (narcotics dispensing, alert overrides, chart edits, ePHI exports), logs record only the shared moniker, creating an accountability void. Naive heuristics resolve only 43.90% of actions under overlapping shifts. This academic proof of concept eliminates this void via multi-signal attribution. On synthetic data (18 staff, 6 shared accounts, 26 delegations, 10 privileged action types, 364 total events, 205 sensitive actions), prototype attribution reached 99.02% (203/205) vs 43.90% baseline (90/205), delivering a +55.12 percentage-point lift with 2/205 (0.98%) escalated. Project requirement coverage is 103/113 = 91.15%, exceeding the 70% Review #2 gate. All 39 tests passed (0 failures). All three Review #1 gaps are remediated.

2. PROBLEM AND OBJECTIVE
In emergency, ICU, and surgical suites, individual logins introduce unacceptable delays during urgent care. Consequently, hospitals deploy shared terminal accounts. However, audit logs record only the shared moniker, concealing who dispensed medications, modified records, or bypassed alerts.
The objective is to build and validate a local academic proof of concept attributing sensitive actions under shared accounts to individual human identities using available forensic evidence (delegations, sessions, device fingerprints, subnets). The system handles delayed, duplicate, and degraded telemetry while escalating ambiguous cases to human review.

3. IMPLEMENTED SOLUTION
The architecture comprises eleven modular components:
- Multi-Source Event Ingestion: Ingests application, auth, session, delegation, and network telemetry.
- Normalized 17-Field Event Model: Standardizes logs into canonical NormalizedEvent schema.
- Delegation Lifecycle: Tracks CREATED, ACTIVE, EXPIRED, REVOKED, and CANCELLED states.
- Session Lifecycle: Tracks CREATED, ACTIVE, IDLE, ENDED, EXPIRED, and TERMINATED states.
- Evidence Model: Rubric (+40 delegation, +30 session, +15 device, +10 subnet, +5 dept) classifying dossiers (STRONG, SUPPORTING, MISSING, CONFLICTING, INSUFFICIENT).
- Delayed/Out-of-Order Handling: In-memory queue (30s window) for sequencing and late reconciliation.
- SHA-256 Duplicate Detection: Rejects duplicate payloads at intake boundary.
- Tamper-Evident Audit Chain: Cryptographically links decisions and reviews in a SHA-256 ledger.
- Telemetry Resilience: Maintains attribution using available credentials when telemetry degrades.
- Human-in-the-Loop Escalation: Queues ambiguous actions for compliance officer review.
- Compliance Dashboard: 17-section Streamlit console for audit and persona review.

4. REVIEW #1 REMEDIATION
All three Review #1 evaluator gaps were fully remediated and verified:
- Gap A: Delayed/out-of-order events and late delegation reconciliation (src/ingestion_buffer.py). 15 sensitive actions held PENDING were retroactively reconciled upon late delegation arrival (15/15 reconciled = 100%).
- Gap B: Missing telemetry/device/CIDR/user-agent resilience (src/telemetry_stress.py). Six telemetry levels tested from 100% to 0%. At 0% telemetry, credential-supported actions remain attributed; uncertain cases are escalated without guessing.
- Gap C: Human-in-loop escalation and formal error analysis (src/escalation_service.py). Unresolved cases escalated to compliance officers with a 13-point taxonomy and 5 adjudication actions; 2 high-priority incidents triaged.

7. ERROR ANALYSIS AND HUMAN REVIEW
Ambiguous, conflicting, or missing evidence is not force-attributed; such cases are escalated for compliance review. Two cases were escalated (results/error_analysis_v2.csv):
- CASE_2026_001 (EVT_SYNTH_0199): CHANGE_TREATMENT_PLAN on radiology_shared. Conflicting delegations between Dr. Lin (U002) and Dr. Reed (U007) with identical telemetry. Classified as AMBIGUOUS.
- CASE_2026_002 (EVT_SYNTH_0245): EMERGENCY_ACCESS_OVERRIDE on er_triage_shared. Session referenced user ID U999, absent from staff roster. Classified as UNATTRIBUTED.
Compliance officers adjudicate cases using five actions (CONFIRM_IDENTITY, MARK_UNATTRIBUTED, REQUEST_MORE_EVIDENCE, DISMISS, ESCALATE) recorded in the audit ledger.
"Attribution identifies the most defensible identity based on available evidence; it does not establish intent, misconduct, or legal responsibility."

8. ETHICS AND LIMITATIONS
The project operates under explicit academic boundaries (docs/ethics_and_limitations.md):
- Synthetic data only: 18 staff, 6 shared accounts, 26 delegations, 364 events. No real patient data.
- Academic proof of concept: In-memory evaluation with no production EHR integration.
- Attribution is not intent or legal responsibility: Contextual evidence does not determine legal culpability.
- Telemetry can be missing or spoofed: Network CIDRs, fingerprints, and user-agents are supporting signals.
- Uncertain cases require human review: Mandatory human review prior to any compliance action.
- Production deployment requires additional security, privacy, governance, access-control and compliance validation.
